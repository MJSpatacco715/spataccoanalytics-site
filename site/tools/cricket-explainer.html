Everything you need to know, translated for American sports fans

⚾

Baseball Diamond

  • ▸Diamond shaped infield with 4 bases
  • ▸Runners circle bases counter-clockwise
  • ▸Pitcher throws from raised mound
🏏

Cricket Oval

  • ▸Oval shaped with rectangular pitch in center
  • ▸Two batters run back and forth between wickets
  • ▸Bowler delivers from ground level with bounce

Key Difference: Baseball has foul territory—hit it there, it doesn't count. In cricket, you can hit the ball in any direction, 360° around you.

🎯 Pitching vs Bowling

Baseball Pitcher
✓ Throws from elevated mound
✓ Ball arrives on the fly (no bounce)
✓ Uses curveballs, sliders, changeups
✓ Arm motion can be overhand, sidearm
Cricket Bowler
✓ Takes a running start (run-up)
✓ Ball must bounce before batter
✓ Arm must stay straight (no bent elbow)
✓ Spin off the pitch creates deception

Key Difference: A cricket bowler's arm must stay straight—bending it is illegal (called "chucking"). Imagine pitching with a stiff arm while running. That's bowling.

How Runs Work

1
Single Run

Both batters swap ends once. Like reaching first base.

↔️
2-3
Multiple Runs

Keep running back and forth until fielder retrieves ball. Like extra bases.

↔️↔️
4
Boundary (Ground)

Ball rolls/bounces to rope at field edge. Automatic 4 runs. Like a ground-rule double, but better.

〰️
6
Six (Over the Boundary)

Ball clears rope on the fly. Cricket's home run. Maximum excitement.

🚀

Typical Scores

5 - 3

🏏 Cricket Match (T20)
185 - 171

Key Difference: Cricket scores look like basketball scores. A single batter might score 50-100 runs in one innings if they're good enough.

🎯
Bowled
≈ Strikeout (sort of)

Ball hits the wicket behind you. Most dramatic way to get out.

🧤
Caught
≈ Fly out

Fielder catches your hit before it bounces. Exact same as baseball.

🦵
LBW
≈ No equivalent

Leg Before Wicket—ball would have hit wicket but hit your body instead. You're out.

🏃
Run Out
≈ Thrown out at base

Fielder breaks wicket while you're running between ends. Very similar.

⚡
Stumped
≈ Catcher tag out

You step forward to hit, miss, and wicket-keeper breaks wicket behind you.

🤦
Hit Wicket
≈ No equivalent

You accidentally knock over your own wicket while batting. Embarrassing.

Key Difference: In baseball, 3 strikes = out. In cricket, there are no strikes or balls. Miss 50 times? You're still batting. But one mistake that hits that wicket? Game over for you.

Traditional

Test Cricket

5 DAYS
Yes, really

The original format. Each team bats twice. No limit on overs. Games can end in a draw if time runs out. Think of it as chess—slow, strategic, grueling.

Purist favorite International only
Limited Overs

One Day International (ODI)

~8 HRS
50 overs per side

Each team gets exactly 50 overs (300 legal deliveries) to score as much as possible. World Cup format. Good balance of strategy and action.

World Cup format Full day event
Fast & Modern

T20 (Twenty20)

~3 HRS
20 overs per side

This is what you should watch first. Fast-paced, big hits, constant action. Same length as a baseball game. IPL (India) and Big Bash (Australia) are the big leagues.
