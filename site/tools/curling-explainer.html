CURLING VS CORNHOLE

You already know this game. It's just on ice, with 42-pound granite rocks, and Olympic-level strategy.

🥌 Curling
The Sheet
A 146-foot strip of meticulously prepared ice. At each end sits the House—four concentric rings (12 ft diameter) with a center dot called the Button. You throw from one end toward the far House.
↔
🌽 Cornhole
The Court
A 27-foot lane with a board at each end. You stand behind your board and throw toward the opponent's board. The target is the center hole on the opposite platform.
Key visual: The House in curling is basically the cornhole board — it's the target zone you're aiming for. The Button (dead center) is the hole. Except instead of 27 feet of grass, you've got 126 feet of ice between you and the target.
⬡ Key Difference

There's a House at both ends of the sheet. Teams alternate throwing toward the far end's House, then physically walk to that end to score and throw back. In cornhole, both boards stay put — in curling, the active target alternates each "End" (round).

How You Score — Cancellation Scoring
This is the single most important concept. Both curling and cornhole use cancellation scoring — only one team scores per round, and your opponent's best stone/bag cancels out yours.

Holes cancel (3–3=0), boards cancel (1–1=0). Cancellation scoring.
The "Blank End" move: If you have the last stone advantage (the hammer) but can't score 2+ points, elite teams intentionally finish an End with zero score for both teams — a "blank end." This preserves the hammer for the next end. In cornhole terms: deliberately tying 0–0 to keep "last bag" advantage.
Point Values
🥌 Curling
1 pt per stone in the House
Each of your stones closer to the Button than your opponent's nearest stone = 1 point. A score of 5 in one end is a big deal — it means you had 5 stones in the House and opponent had zero inside them.
↔
🌽 Cornhole
3 pts hole / 1 pt on board
In the hole = 3 points. On the board = 1 point. Bags that fall off count as zero. Net scoring only — opponent's points cancel yours.
⬡ Key Difference

Curling doesn't have different point values for stone placement — every stone in the House is worth exactly 1 point, provided it's closer to the Button than your opponent's nearest stone. The House doesn't have a hole — the Button is just the reference point. The stone touching the Button isn't automatically worth more; it's just the most protected position.

The Throw — Delivery
1

The Hack

You start in the Hack — a rubber foothold embedded in the ice. You push off from it like a starting block. Cornhole equivalent: Standing behind the board.

2

The Slide & Release

You glide forward on one knee, balanced on a Teflon-soled shoe, holding the stone's handle. Must release before the Hog Line (a red line at ~26 ft). Releasing late = stone removed. Cornhole equivalent: You have to release the bag before your foot crosses the foul line.

3

The Turn (The Curl)

At release, you rotate the handle clockwise or counter-clockwise. This creates the curl — the stone arcs toward the House rather than traveling straight. Clockwise = curls right. Counter = curls left. This is the entire skill of the sport. Cornhole equivalent: Backspin vs. topspin to control whether a bag slides or sticks.

4

Sweeping

This is the part that looks insane to first-timers. Two teammates sprint ahead of the thrown stone and furiously sweep the ice with brooms. Sweeping melts a thin layer of ice, reducing friction — making the stone travel faster, farther, and curl less. The Skip (captain) calls "Sweep!" or "Hard!" to direct them. Cornhole equivalent: Imagine if, after you tossed a bag, your teammate could slightly redirect it in mid-air. That's sweeping.

The "curl" is where the name comes from. Every stone curves. How much it curves (6–18 inches typically) depends on ice temperature, ice texture (pebble), weight of throw, and amount of handle rotation. Reading the ice — knowing exactly how much a stone will curl on a given sheet — is an elite skill.
Shot Types
Draw
Land softly in the House
A finesse shot — just enough weight to reach and stop inside the House, ideally near the Button. The bread-and-butter shot. Cornhole: tossing your bag gently so it lands and sticks on the board.
↔
Takeout
Knock the opponent out
A power shot — throw hard to knock your opponent's stone(s) out of the House. Both stones typically slide off the far end. Cornhole: throwing hard to knock opponent's bag off the board.
The Raise: Hitting one of your own stones to bump it into a better position — like nudging your own cornhole bag closer to the hole after it landed on the board.
⬡ Key Difference

In cornhole, once the bag leaves your hand, it's done — you just watch. In curling, the throw isn't over until the stone stops. Sweeping is active, real-time shot-shaping that happens after release. A stone that looks like it's going wide can be swept back on line. This is why curling is sometimes called "chess on ice" — the strategy continues mid-throw.

The Hammer — Last Stone Advantage
🥌 Curling — The Hammer
Last stone of the End
The team with the Hammer throws the final stone of the End. This is a massive advantage — like last ups in baseball. The team that scores an End loses the Hammer for the next End. The team that gets blanked (scoreless) retains the Hammer.
↔
🌽 Cornhole — Last Bag
Last toss of the round
Whoever scores in a round typically hasn't "earned" formal last-bag advantage in standard rules, but tournament cornhole tracks throwing order. Throwing last is always the best position.
⬡ Key Difference

Cornhole is typically 2v2 or 1v1. Curling is always 4v4, and the division of labor is formalized. The Skip doesn't just throw — they actively coach the stone after it's released by shouting commands to the sweepers. This real-time communication is fundamental to the sport, not optional.

Game Structure — Ends
🥌 Curling — "Ends"
Basically Innings
A standard competitive game has 10 Ends. Each End: both teams alternate throwing all 8 of their stones. Score the End. Clear the House. Walk to the other end and repeat. Highest score after 10 Ends wins. If tied, play extra Ends (one at a time) until someone scores.
↔
🌽 Cornhole — "Rounds"
Rounds to 21
You play rounds until someone reaches exactly 21 points (or wins by 2 in some rulesets). Each round: both players throw all 4 bags, score net. No fixed number of rounds.

Format Variations
Olympic / Elite
10 Ends (~2.5 hrs)
Full format with thinking time per shot. Used at Olympics, World Championships, Slams. The clock matters — teams get ~38 minutes of "thinking time" total, tracked per throw.
→
Club / Rec League
8 Ends (~1.5 hrs)
Most club leagues play 8 ends. Same rules, just shorter. Like playing cornhole to 15 instead of 21.
The Concession: In curling, it is completely normal and sportsmanlike to concede (forfeit) mid-game when the math makes a comeback impossible. If you're down 8 points with 3 ends left, you shake hands and walk off. No one thinks less of you. This would never fly in cornhole.
⬡ Key Difference

Cornhole is a race to a target score. Curling is a fixed-length format — 10 Ends, no matter what (unless tied). This means curling has explicit game management: teams track stolen ends, hammer efficiency, and percentage of shots made. The Olympics uses a shot clock. It's a slower, more deliberate sport — but once you understand the strategy layer, every single stone has meaning.
